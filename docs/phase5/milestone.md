## Self-Assessment: Job-Ready Levels

### Beginner (Not Ready Yet — Need 1-2 More Weeks)

**Indicators:**
- Portfolio incomplete (projects without complete READMEs)
- No demo videos
- Resume still generic
- Mock interviews score <50%
- Can't confidently answer 80% of conceptual questions

**Example:** "I have projects but the READMEs are minimal and I haven't recorded demos. I get nervous in interviews."

**Action:** Spend 2 more weeks polishing. Don't start job search yet; you'll waste time on rejections.

### Competent (Ready for Job Market — Go for It)

**Indicators:**
- 3-4 strong projects on GitHub
- Complete READMEs with metrics
- Loom demos visible
- Resume tailored to AI roles
- Mock interview score 70%+
- Can answer most conceptual questions
- Know your target companies

**Example:** "Portfolio is strong, resume highlights my best work with metrics.  I'm prepared for interviews and know what I'm looking for. Ready to apply."

**Action:** Start applying. Target 50-100 applications. Expect 5-10% callback rate.

### Hire-Ready (Stand Out — Expect Multiple Offers)

**Indicators:**
- Portfolio exceptional (projects have 50+ stars, shipped to production)
- Metrics impressive (RAGAS scores, latency numbers, cost optimization)
- Mock interview score 80%+
- Can handle all behavioral questions smoothly
- Active in AI community (Twitter, Discord, visible)
- Have warm connections at target companies
- Know market extremely well, negotiate confidently

**Example:** "Portfolio is production-grade — systems I built hold real users and metrics. I have referrals at 3 dream companies. Done system design interviews. Confident I'll get offers from tier-1 companies."

**Action:** Apply strategically. You can afford to be picky. Aim for best team/equity, not highest salary.

## The Job Search Process

### Week 1: Final Prep

```
Mon: Polish README for 2-3 key projects
Tue: Record Loom demos
Wed: Resume final edit, LinkedIn push
Thu: Mock interview (identify weak areas)
Fri: Update target company list
```

**By Friday:** Fully ready to apply.

### Week 2: Apply

```
Mon-Wed: Send 10-15 applications/day
(Customize cover letter for each!)

Thu-Fri: Follow up with warm connections
(LinkedIn message: "Just applied, thought you should know!")
```

**Target: 50-100 applications by Friday.**

### Week 3: Interviews Start

```
Expect: Callbacks from first 20-30 applications
First calls: Recruiter screen (30 min) → schedule deeper interviews
Pace: 2-3 interviews per day on average
```

### Week 4: Offers Arrive

```
Company A: Offer received (counter-offer template ready)
Company B: Final interviews scheduled
Company C: Rejected (OK, move on)

Typical: 20% callback → 30% pass phone screen → 40% pass final → 50% offer

So 100 apps → 20 phone screens → 6 pass → 3 offers → pick best
```

### Week 5-6: Decision & Negotiation

```
You likely have 2-3 offers
Negotiate each (use template from Phase 5)
Choose best fit (not just salary)
Give notice at current job
```

❌ **Portfolio is incomplete**
- "I have the projects but README is just code"

- "I know machine learning and Python"

❌ **Can't discuss your own projects in detail**

- Fix: Go back to your code. Own it. Understand every decision.

❌ **No way to reach you**
- Email hard to find, LinkedIn incomplete, GitHub profile bare
- Fix: Make it easy. Bio links, consistent username everywhere.

## Job Search Red Flags

### Company Red Flags (Skip These)

- Posting for "AI engineer" but vague requirements (don't know what they want)
- Salary posted as "competitive" (not a number = pay less)
- No mention of team size or tech stack (chaotic org)
- "Move fast and break things" culture (burnout city)
- VCs, not product (research jobs, not production)
- Required on-site for "company culture" (inflexible, probably bad)

### Interview Red Flags

- No portfolio discussion (they don't care about your work)
- All questions from LeetCode (no system design = junior company)
- Asked personal questions (age, marital status, etc. = illegal + immature)
- Vague about the actual role (bait and switch)
- No technical depth from interviewers (they can't evaluate you fairly)

## Expected Outcomes

### Timeline (Realistic)

```
Day 1: Decide to start job search
Days 1-3: Final portfolio prep
Days 4-11: 100 applications sent
Days 12-17: First callbacks
Days 18-31: Interview loop (sometimes multiple)
Days 32-38: Offer negotiations
Days 39-45: Accepted role, give notice
Days 46-60: Transition period
Day 60+: First day at new job
```

**Total: 2-3 months for full cycle.**

### Callback Rates (Our Experience)

```
100 cold applications → 5-10 callbacks (5-10%)
With referrals: 50 applications → 10-15 callbacks (20-30%)

Callbacks that pass phone screen: 30-40%
Phone screens that pass final loop: 40-50%
Final loops that extend offers: 50-70%

So 100 cold apps → 5 callbacks → 2 phone passes → 1 final → 0.5 offers
With 50 referrals → 15 callbacks → 6 phone passes → 3 finals → 2 offers
```

**Key:** Referrals are 4x better than cold applications.

## First Job in AI Role: What's Normal

### Month 1: Onboarding

- Meet team, understand codebase
- You'll feel dumb ("How does this work?") ← normal
- Pair with senior engineer
- First project: small, self-contained

### Month 3: Ramping

- Completing projects without help
- Understanding team dynamics
- Building relationships
- Prove yourself reliable

### Month 6: Contributing

- Leading small projects
- Mentoring others (even if just 1 person)
- Opinions are respected
- Consider asking for raise (often not automatic)

### Month 12: Strategic

- Clear growth path in your mind
- Company's evaluation of you is clear (good/bad/neutral)
- Decide: stay and push for promotion, or job search?
